# Current limits

[Canonical positioning and claim-to-evidence guide](POSITIONING.md). John is an illustrative buyer, not an external customer. The example agent is deterministic, not an LLM shopping or browsing autonomously; its credentials cannot approve a purchase or sign transfers. RouteTill is not custodial escrow, an installed Shopify/WooCommerce plugin, or a proprietary bridge. No AP2, Chain Signatures or NEAR AI inference integration is claimed.

- **Verified achievement:** one genuine restricted Arbitrum USDC → Base USDC purchase reached FULFILLED through normal reconciliation. Exactly 10 Base USDC, one entitlement, 1,613 matching bytes and one signed Passport were independently verified. [Reproduce the proof](evidence/mainnet-655d4cc9-0eaf-41b7-a7bf-05fc73286eea/REPRODUCE.txt).
- **General live checkout is not enabled.** The single-order trial guard still requires exact order/policy/identity/asset/amount/digest bindings and valid human approval. Both deployments keep live preparation disabled. Ordinary public payment activation needs a separately reviewed architecture; the trial’s per-order deployment is not represented as normal customer operation.
- **Public demo uses no funds.** Guest checkout and the five Lab scenarios exercise actual policy validation, SQL transactions, entitlement access and Passport checks with simulated payment evidence. Demo FULFILLED means demo delivery, not blockchain settlement. Production/demo Neon stores and issuer keys remain distinct.
- One independently verified route: native USDC on Arbitrum → native USDC on Base. A wider provider catalog does not imply support for other RouteTill verification routes.
- Recipient ownership uses a nonce-bound EIP-191 message; EIP-1271 contract-wallet ownership is unsupported. Direct injected Rabby, MetaMask and OKX do not require WalletConnect. Provider coexistence and rejection tests use mocks; the genuine trial used the owner’s Rabby extension. Real WalletConnect pairing and physical-device testing remain unverified.
- Buyers use authenticated accounts for live approval and private download access. The current account model is shared with merchant accounts; no separate buyer-role authentication system is claimed. The public prepared demo needs no account, owner code or wallet. Guest sessions and budgets are intentionally bounded.
- Digital products are UTF-8 text/Markdown up to 16 KB. SHA-256 establishes exact bytes, not quality or off-platform delivery. Entitlements permit authorized repeat downloads; “exactly once” refers to entitlement issuance, not a limit of one HTTP download.
- Human approval and database uniqueness are application assertions, not a buyer blockchain signature. The issuer signature protects the Passport’s contents; issuer identity requires independently pinning the public key.
- Independent RPC observations do not verify Ethereum consensus or provide receipt inclusion proofs. Provider status associates deposits and destination references but is not a cryptographic causal-routing proof.
- The source cap covers USDC input. Arbitrum ETH gas is additional and may change. The effective funding deadline is the earliest policy/request/provider deadline; a later provider date never extends the immutable purchase. Consumed or expired approvals cannot be reused.
- Pending, incomplete, failed, refunded and disputed payments are not successful delivery. The verified purchase’s separate source-chain refund was independently checked and did not replace the required merchant receipt.
- Local PGlite supports one process per store. Real Neon concurrent approval, rollback, isolation and reconnect tests have a dated passing [report](evidence/neon-verification.json); WSL DNS/socket reliability is intermittent. Hosted replay is distinct evidence.
- No password reset/email verification, arbitrary binary storage, automatic dispute resolution, regulatory certification or automated merchant webhook delivery is claimed. Schedulers are best effort; durable reconciliation/backoff/outbox still govern fulfillment.
